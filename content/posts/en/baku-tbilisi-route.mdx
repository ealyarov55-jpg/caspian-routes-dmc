## By Air: The Only Reliable Option

Due to land border restrictions, airlines currently hold a monopoly on this route.

- **Airlines:** AZAL (Azerbaijan Airlines) and Georgian Airways
- **Flight time:** 1 hour 10 minutes
- **Average cost (round trip):** 320–450 AZN ($190–265). Spikes to 600 AZN during holidays
- **Tip:** Book at least 3–4 weeks in advance. Weekend flights run at near 100% capacity

## The Baku–Tbilisi Train: 2026 Reality

There is a lot of misinformation about the Baku–Tbilisi–Kars (BTK) railway.

- **Current status:** Passenger service is strictly suspended
- **Why:** Although infrastructure is ready for Stadler trains, Azerbaijan's "special quarantine regime" keeps land borders sealed for travelers
- **Verdict:** Don't plan a train journey in 2026 — those trains are staying in the depot until borders fully reopen

---

## Bus & Private Car: One-Way Only

- **The Red Bridge (Qırmızı Körpü):** Open only for cargo trucks and specific diplomatic missions
- **Exiting Azerbaijan:** Foreign nationals can sometimes get permission to leave via land, but cannot re-enter the same way
- **Entering Azerbaijan by land:** Strictly prohibited for all tourists. Do not book a bus from Tbilisi to Baku — it will be stopped at the border

## Tbilisi in 48 Hours: The Gold Standard

If you're making the trip, here's what to prioritize:

- **Abanotubani** — sulfur baths district and waterfall in the city center. Bath from 20 GEL/hour
- **Narikala Fortress** — cable car from Rike Park for the best panoramic views. 2.5 GEL
- **Mtskheta** — ancient capital, 20 minutes from Tbilisi. UNESCO World Heritage site. Taxi there and back ~30 GEL
- **Food:** Khinkali 1.5–3 GEL each, Adjaruli Khachapuri (the iconic cheese boat), Saperavi wine
